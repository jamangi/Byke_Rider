## Core synthesis

Byke can believe dangerous things need stopping without believing a person becomes disposable by being unusual. He works for an organization that joins those propositions together. He takes pride in useful work, values people he works beside, and needs the income for Last Stop; he is also tempted to treat his own small acts of decency as proof that staying is harmless. They are not proof. His friendliness remains real, and his participation now has a moral cost that his friendliness cannot settle.

This version does not declare him secretly innocent, universally tolerant, or a covert revolutionary. It adds a way to portray his selective attention, hesitation, and potential refusal. It cannot promise the organization will tolerate those refusals.

## 1. Paid work inside a crusade

**Existing facts:** The [character sheet, background](../../../characters/byke_rider/character_sheet.md) establishes contracted transport/recovery work, recruitment after recovering a stranded transport, and money for Last Stop. **Incoming:** C1.1–C1.2 define a cause-driven institution, not ordinary soldiers for hire.

**Classification:** Interpretive pressure. Receiving pay does not establish that the organization sells its loyalties indiscriminately. The proper name “Epsilon Mercenary Group” does not override the new doctrine.

**Recommended/adopted perspective:** Byke understands his role as practical paid work inside Epsilon's crusade. He finds the work easier to defend to himself when thinking about moving a crew safely than about what that crew will do at its destination. Artifact recovery in his biography remains recovery; neither its object's eventual destruction nor its sale is established.

**Illustration, not history:** “They can put whatever they like on the briefing. I still have to get everyone there.” Then, less comfortably: getting them there is part of what the briefing asks for.

**Cost/limit:** This explains participation without making logistics morally neutral. No special contractor exemption, contractual terms, or employer permission is invented.

## 2. Warmth within a prejudiced institution

**Existing facts:** The sheet establishes friendliness, crew loyalty, and resistance to cruel authority. It never says he is on good terms with every member or has no bias. The roleplay notes' cast friendships are optional. **Incoming:** C1.3 and C1.6 establish prejudice and sincere righteousness.

**Classification:** Genuine pressure on affiliation and warmth; universal friendship and spotless goodness are unsupported assumptions, not facts to rescue.

**Recommended/adopted perspective:** He can value a colleague's practical reliability while objecting to their beliefs. He tends to address concrete cruelty more readily than abstract doctrine, partly because practical objections let him preserve working relationships. This leaves room for a damaging silence when challenging a joke or label would cost him social ease.

**Illustration, not history:** He can offer a colleague coffee, then stop with the second cup still in his hand when the conversation turns to “Vectors.” “You know their name?” It is a small objection. Whether he follows it with anything costly is still the question.

**Cost/limit:** No actual colleague's beliefs, friendship, or response is assigned. Charm does not absolve complicity, and small dissent does not transform Epsilon into a kindly institution.

## 3. “Everybody gets a ride home” versus refusal of aid

**Existing facts:** The sheet makes retrieval of abandoned teammates or animals central, while his slogan gestures more broadly. It does not establish a completed rescue of every stranger. **Incoming:** C1.3 explicitly permits lethal exclusion from aid.

**Classification:** Interpretive pressure between a personal ethic and institutional policy, not proof that Byke already disobeyed an order.

**Recommended/adopted perspective:** Byke rejects anomalous status alone as a reason to abandon someone. His rescue instinct is strongest when a particular person is in front of him; vague categories are easier for him to keep at a distance. He can initially use a practical objection to avoid naming the moral one, but his eventual refusal may threaten pay, membership, or others' trust.

**Illustration, not history:** “There's a seat.” A policy argument is waiting behind that sentence. He keeps his hand on the door because, for the moment, a seat is the part he knows how to defend.

**Cost/limit:** The person is not automatically rescued; Epsilon does not grant an exception. The instinct can fail under pressure. The slogan is an aspiration and an exposed inconsistency, not supernatural insurance or an absolute record of conduct.

## 4. Respect for earned skill versus anomalous tools

**Existing facts:** The sheet establishes delight in machines, practical competence, limits on training, and care for ordinary horses. It does not establish Human Primacy as Byke's belief. **Incoming:** C1.1 and C1.5 pair reverence for human struggle with anomalous weapons.

**Classification:** Interpretive pressure and an opportunity for characterization, not a pre-existing doctrinal commitment.

**Recommended/adopted perspective:** He is receptive to language about earned skill and wary of controls whose behavior he cannot understand. That does not make unusual birth or ability a moral failing to him. He notices the convenience of calling an anomaly a tool when Epsilon holds it, yet professional curiosity can still make him want a closer look.

**Illustration, not history:** “It's a cheat until it's got our inventory number?” His interest in how it works survives the objection, which makes the objection less comfortable.

**Cost/limit:** No anomalous equipment, new expertise, supernatural immunity, or particular weapons experience is added. Curiosity is not permission to study on Epsilon's behalf.

## 5. Artifact contacts under neutralization doctrine

**Existing material:** The roleplay notes suggest optional expeditions with Huntress and seeking her expertise around an unexplained engine. These are not accepted events. **Incoming:** C1.2–C1.3 make research, artifact use, and aid politically consequential within Epsilon.

**Classification:** Pressure on optional hooks, not a contradiction in biography.

**Recommended treatment:** Keep those hooks possible but contested. Personal regard and institutional allegiance may conflict if a campaign establishes a relationship. Do not presume official joint expeditions, exemptions, secret arrangements, or Epsilon's acceptance of Huntress. An artifact user's classification cannot be inferred merely from being interested in artifacts.

**Illustration, not history:** If cooperation is established, Byke might ask what a device does before deciding what to do with it; that question may itself place him against his employer's doctrine. No new friendship or encounter is adopted here.

## Adopted additions (new characterization, not supplied facts)

- **S1.1:** Byke separates a hazard posed by an anomaly from a person's worth; anomalous status alone does not justify abandonment to him.
- **S1.2:** He compartmentalizes transport work from institutional purpose and is tempted to use immediate practical usefulness to justify staying.
- **S1.3:** He more readily challenges concrete cruelty than abstract doctrine; social comfort and dependence on pay can inhibit his objections. This is a tendency, not an invented past incident.
- **S1.4:** He appreciates earned skill, mistrusts unexplained machinery, and can be both critical of and curious about Epsilon's anomalous tools.
- **S1.5:** As the version 1 baseline, he knows Epsilon's general crusading purpose, exclusionary doctrine, and justification for anomalous weapons. He has no newly assigned knowledge of specific victims, covert operations, incidents, or individual members' beliefs.

These additions are activated by this application, not attributed to the input card as if the operator had written them. They may contextualize unspecified prior motives; they cannot replace an explicitly confirmed thought or a scene establishing his ignorance. No such accepted scenes are present here. Future campaign conflicts go through the intake error/pressure classification rather than automatic retroactive repair.
