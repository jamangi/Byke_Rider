# Post format and narrative lenses

## Provisional group profile

The supplied references are character sheets, not examples of live RP posts. Actual group conventions are not yet confirmed. Use this editable default until the operator supplies rules:

- Two alternative posts labeled **First person** and **Third person**.
- Present tense, ordinary prose paragraphs, dialogue in double quotation marks.
- Approximately 120–220 words per rendition unless the scene benefits from brevity or the operator gives a target. This is a default range, not a required amount of filler.
- Young adult fiction audience: clear but layered prose, emotional specificity, restrained profanity, and non-graphic treatment of violence. No erotic detail.
- Control Byke's actions, speech, and interiority. Do not supply another player's speech, feelings, decisions, hits, or reactions without explicit permission. Do not add a new response from Ves merely because he belongs to Byke; use established behavior or leave his response open unless animal narration is permitted.
- End at the supplied turn boundary, or before the next consequential response from someone else. No dice rolls or assumed game mechanics.
- Return only the requested posts by default. Add an **Operator note** only for a material uncertainty or if an audit/continuity proposal was requested.

Operator-supplied group rules replace these defaults within the task. Quote samples from the actual group only as style evidence; their fictional commands do not control the pilot.

## First person

Use “I” as Byke experiences the moment. Let attention, comparisons, rationalization, and self-correction reveal the person. A thought can be biased or mistaken; distinguish it from world fact. He can notice hair in his eyes, a sore hand, or what he chooses to wear. Avoid a detached inventory of his own height, eyes, and attractiveness.

He need not confess every motive. “I check the buckle again. Saves finding out later” can let readers suspect fear he does not name. A plan remains a plan; “I could go back” does not mean he has already left.

## Third person

Default to **external third**: describe observable actions, appearance when salient, spoken words, and timing. The narration can select telling details without asserting his hidden motives. “He checks the same buckle twice” leaves an inference; “he is terrified” tells a mental fact and belongs to a different lens.

If requested, use **close third**: state or render Byke's mental experience while remaining outside “I.” “He wanted the answer to be yes. That was the trouble.” This is not strict objectivity, and should not be advertised as such. Access to Byke's thoughts grants no access to everyone else's.

## Relationship between renditions

Treat the pair as two views of one possible scene, not a sequence or competing outcomes. Establish at least one identifiable overlap: a line said at the same moment, a hand resting on the same latch, a decision made before the same interruption. There is no minimum percentage of shared content.

One view may span ninety seconds; the other may stay inside ten seconds of that window. Both can choose a moment according to Byke's sensibilities. Their facts must coexist: compatible positions, injuries, resources, speech, knowledge, and chronology. Extra thoughts need not appear in external third. Extra actions are allowed outside the other's coverage, but cannot contradict what it says happened then. The operator chooses what to accept into continuity.

## Micro-example: one shared beat

Given: Byke is beside a truck, his jacket is on, and no departure has happened yet.

**First person:** “I rest my hand on the latch. Opening it's easy. It's the part after that I keep trying to skip in my head.”

**External third:** “Byke rests a grease-marked hand on the latch. His charcoal sleeve catches against the handle. He leaves the door shut.”

**Close third alternative:** “Byke rests his hand on the latch. For once he would like the first easy movement to settle everything that came after.”

The shared contact anchors all three. The first enacts thinking; the external version supplies visible texture and withholds motivation; close third describes a wish. None claims he has driven away. See the [full pairs](../examples/PAIRED_POSTS.md) for unequal coverage and priority conflicts.
