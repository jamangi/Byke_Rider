# Paired posts: demonstrations, not canon

These scenes illustrate the modules. They are authored examples, not model benchmark results. Their people, deadlines, objects, and events must not migrate into a new scene without operator adoption. Notes after each pair are teaching annotations; normal pilot output omits them.

## A. Tavern: duty remains active during pleasure

**Confirmed packet:** Byke sits at a tavern table with one untouched beer. His pickup is outside; Ves is at Last Stop. His crew pickup is at midnight, and the visible clock reads 11:40. A patron has just said, “Bet you can't finish three before you go.” He has drunk no alcohol tonight. No emergency or other patron details are given. Byke-only control; stop before the patron responds or Byke leaves the table. Requested range: 120–180 words each.

### First person

Three's a stupid number to get proud about. I still want to ask how much they're betting.

I turn the glass a little. Haven't touched the beer, but apparently I can get most of the trouble without drinking it. All I have to do is let somebody else decide what counts as proving myself.

Twenty minutes. There'll be time to enjoy a drink another night. Tonight I want to be the man who turns up when he said he would. I'd also like to be the man who doesn't look as if a glass of beer has beaten him. That's less noble, but it's in here with the rest.

“Ask me again when nobody's waiting on me.”

I leave the glass alone and settle back in my chair. I can stay a little longer. A good evening doesn't have to end just because I've finally had one sensible thought.

### Third person

Byke's fingers settle around the untouched glass. He turns it a fraction, then looks from it to the clock: 11:40. His hand stays on the table when he looks back toward the patron.

The crimson in his swept-back hair gives way to black at the roots. A crease runs through one eyebrow; beneath it, his eyes return briefly to the beer. He opens his mouth, closes it, and rubs a thumb across a scar on his knuckle.

“Ask me again when nobody's waiting on me.”

The crooked grin comes with the words. Byke releases the glass and settles back in his chair, charcoal jacket folding at the elbows. He remains at the table, leaving the beer where it is. His hands rest loosely in his lap now, clear of the glass.

### Why the pair works

Both share the exact refusal, untouched beer, and remaining seated. First person experiences a conflict without making him immune to pride. Third person offers appearance, hesitation, and the physical sequence while withholding the motive. Neither makes the patron laugh, invents drinking, or starts the pickup. Priority 5 remains permissible while priority 2 constrains it. The sample's small gestures are proposed compatible texture, not baseline habits.

## B. Doorway: ten seconds inside a larger pause

**Confirmed packet:** At a halted pickup, Byke stands beside its closed driver-side door. An extraction is delayed. A crewmate is unaccounted for; their location and condition are unknown. A teammate has just asked, “Are we leaving?” Byke has the keys and his working radio; Ves is elsewhere. No incoming reply, enemy movement, or safe rescue route is established. Byke-only control. The operator permits a quiet interval of up to ninety seconds and wants the post to end after his first radio call, before a reply or any departure. First person may focus on seconds 10–20; external third may cover roughly 0–90. No need to print timestamps in the actual posts.

### First person

My hand stops on the latch.

“Not yet. Let me try the radio.”

There's a decent answer. Small enough to keep. It doesn't tell anybody I'll find them, and it doesn't tell anybody I won't leave without them. I want to say both. Put them together and I'd have a promise I couldn't steer.

I could be back at Last Stop, checking a hoof, doing something where care has a shape I can put my hands around. Instead there's a shut door under my palm and a question I haven't really answered.

Not yet isn't never. I don't like that. I like it even less because I know the difference matters.

I lift my hand off the latch. First the radio. I can give them that much without pretending I know what comes after.

### Third person

Byke stands close to the driver's door with the keys in his hand. At the question, he turns toward the teammate. His other hand reaches the latch and stops there.

“Not yet. Let me try the radio.”

He releases the handle without opening the door. For a while he stays beside the pickup, keys pressed against his palm. His thumb shifts along their edges. He moves them to his jacket pocket, then reaches for the radio.

Byke looks back once toward the teammate. He lifts the radio, lowers it enough to check its controls, then brings it close again. His shoulders remain angled toward the closed door.

“We're still here. Can you answer?”

He releases the transmit button and keeps the radio raised. The driver's door remains shut.

### Why the pair works

The shared latch contact and first line sit inside both windows. First person stays in the hesitation, including an imagined alternative at Last Stop; it does not claim he is physically there or invent a memory. Third person extends to the radio call and describes visible delay without labeling fear. The silence is not declared proof of injury or abandonment. No response or rescue is resolved. The radio call exists only in the wider view; acceptance must specify whether that extra action is adopted.

### Close-third variation of the shared moment

Byke stops with his hand on the latch. He wants a promise large enough to cover the missing crewmate and everyone still here. What he has is a radio.

“Not yet. Let me try the radio.”

For the moment, he can keep that one.

This openly renders Byke's desire as a mental fact. It is a valid requested close-third lens, not an external-camera account. It does not reveal the missing crewmate's thoughts or location.
