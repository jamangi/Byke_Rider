# Playing Byke Rider

These are optional details and hooks to agree with the group. They do not establish shared history or dictate another player's character.

## Voice and manner

Byke speaks easily, remembers stable hands and mechanics by name, and usually offers help before asking for a favor. His rebellion shows up in crimson hair, irreverent answers, and refusing to let rank excuse cruelty. He enjoys being good at his work and enjoys being seen being good at it almost as much.

When something goes wrong, his jokes get shorter. When he is truly frightened, he starts checking straps and buckles that he already checked. A quiet “You all still with me?” means more than his usual swagger.

- “That's a warning light. It's warning us this'll be expensive.”
- “You can call him transport. I call him Ves. Ask before you touch his ears.”
- “I said I'd driven one. I didn't say this one.”
- “Keep the bonus. Put their names on the passenger list.”

## Small things that make him human

- Carries peppermints for himself and remembers which horses can safely have a treat. Ves prefers apple.
- Repairs the stable's children's bicycles when he comes home. His racing stories become noticeably less accurate with a young audience.
- Keeps a notebook of machines he has operated, with dates, mistakes, and the names of the people who taught him. The submarine pages have the most underlining.
- Refers to a battered but reliable vehicle as “a good old soul.” He still understands that a horse needs care a machine does not.
- Has no gift for artifact research. If an engine starts with no battery, he gets Huntress before he celebrates.

## Optional ties to the example cast

- **Dent:** Byke could be the driver who remembers to leave enough legroom and asks before Dent helps move something fragile. Their friction comes when brute force threatens a machine or frightens a horse; their friendship could grow through straightforward, practical favors.
- **Hex:** Hex could handle security while Byke handles transport. Their most useful disagreement is over whether going back for someone is still possible, and what it will cost. Any history or response involving Hex's trauma belongs to Hex's player.
- **Huntress:** Artifact expeditions need someone to carry awkward cases through inconvenient places. Byke would be fascinated by the guiding cane, then frustrated to discover that a direction is not the same as a passable road.
- **Shimmer / Vesper Nightshade:** Byke's horse shares her first name, a coincidence that could produce a recurring misunderstanding if her player enjoys it. His habit of counting passengers before leaving could collide with her pursuit of one last glittering prize.

## Story hooks

1. **The pasture payment:** A lucrative recovery contract would cover Last Stop's next lease payment, but the client treats the local workers and their animals as expendable. Byke must decide how much compromise the stable is worth.
2. **The borrowed controls:** A contact offers access to an unusual vehicle in exchange for a favor. Byke wants to say yes before asking what it carries, who owns it, or whether he can actually operate it.
3. **One empty seat:** A former salvage colleague goes missing near a flooded recovery site. The last message contains a familiar engine noise and a request to keep the pickup running.

## Visual direction

For an optional future illustrated sheet: use the supplied examples' weathered black panels, cream lettering, thin tarnished-metal borders, and crimson accents. Give Byke a friendly, slightly defiant expression, visible black roots under crimson hair, practical worn riding clothes, and grease-marked hands. Place Ves prominently beside him; machinery can sit in the background without crowding out the horse.

The written character sheet remains the reference for exact text and ratings. No supernatural powers or special artifact are assumed for Byke or Ves.
