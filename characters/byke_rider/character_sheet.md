# CHARACTER SHEET: BYKE RIDER

**Current interpretation: [character version 1.0](../../byke-engine/VERSION.md).** The facts below remain the original baseline. Read the [adopted Epsilon perspective](../../byke-engine/modules/PERSPECTIVE.md) alongside them: his paid transport role operates within a crusading institution, and his care for people now carries a specific tension with its exclusionary doctrine.

> “Everybody gets a ride home. You can argue about the upholstery when we're there.”

## I. BASIC INFORMATION

**Callsign/Code Name:** Byke Rider; usually shortened to Byker.

**Real Name (Optional):** Silas Ryder. “Byke Rider” is the name he chose, and the one he answers to.

**Gender:** Male.

**Age:** 33.

**Nationality:** American, raised in Louisiana.

**Physical Description:** 5'11", 180 lbs, with a lean, sturdy build earned hauling feed, climbing machinery, and spending long days in the saddle. His naturally black hair is dyed vivid crimson, with dark roots he rarely bothers to hide; he wears it swept back or tied clear of his eyes. Hazel eyes, a crooked grin, and a small split through one eyebrow make him look perpetually ready to suggest something inadvisable. He wears a battered charcoal riding jacket, reinforced work trousers, scuffed boots, and a crimson neckerchief. His hands carry grease in the creases and pale scars across the knuckles. The dye began as a refusal to look “respectable” for a bullying foreman; now he simply likes being easy for his friends to spot.

## II. BACKGROUND

**Former Occupation/Specialty:** Stable hand and trail guide; agricultural and dockside equipment operator; long-haul driver; amateur race driver; salvage-submarine pilot's assistant. Specializes in transport, recovery, and difficult extractions.

**Agency/Affiliation:** Epsilon Mercenary Group, contracted transport and recovery specialist. Keeps ties with an independent Louisiana boarding stable called Last Stop.

**Brief Bio (2–3 sentences):** Raised around a struggling boarding stable, Byke learned horses first, then spent his adult life chasing jobs that let him learn another set of controls: tractors and cranes for wages, semi-trailer trucks for distance, bicycles and motorcycles for freedom, and racecars because someone said he couldn't. A coastal salvage job gave him supervised time piloting small work submarines, while an artifact-recovery contract introduced him to Epsilon when he used a farm tractor to pull their stranded transport out of a flooded access road. They offered him regular work after he returned for the last injured contractor, then submitted an invoice that included replacement fencing and feed for the farmer's horses.

**Primary Motivation for taking this mission:** Earn enough to keep Last Stop open and buy its leased pasture before the owner sells. He also wants a reputation as the man who brings the whole crew home, although the chance to drive something extraordinary can make him forget what the money was for.

## IV. SKILLS

1. **Horsemanship and animal handling:** Confident trail rider with a patient touch for nervous horses; reads fatigue, poor footing, and the moment an animal has had enough. Particularly fond of black horses, without mistaking their color for temperament.
2. **Driving and riding:** Highly capable on bicycles, motorcycles, racecars, tractors, and semi-trailer trucks. Strong at controlling a skid, judging clearances, and finding a usable route under pressure; racing experience does not make every surface a racetrack.
3. **Heavy equipment and small-submarine operation:** Practical experience with cranes and agricultural machinery, plus logged supervised piloting of small salvage submarines. Unfamiliar controls require orientation; large submarines, aircraft, and specialist systems remain outside his training.
4. **Field mechanics and recovery:** Diagnoses common mechanical faults, makes temporary repairs, and recovers stuck or disabled vehicles with suitable equipment. He can nurse a damaged engine home, not rebuild one with a pocketknife.
5. **Route finding and extraction:** Reads terrain, plans pickup points, and loads passengers and supplies with escape in mind. Most effective when another teammate handles the shooting while he handles the way out.

## V. GEAR

**Primary Weapon:** Worn lever-action carbine in a secured saddle scabbard or vehicle case. He is a competent defensive shot, not a marksman, and prefers to stop and find cover before using it.

**Secondary Weapon/Tool:** Heavy adjustable wrench, wrapped at the grip with faded crimson tape. A working tool first, a desperate close-range option second.

**Essential Equipment (3 items max):**

1. Compact mechanic's tool roll with basic patching supplies; useful for temporary repairs, not major overhauls.
2. Handheld radio with a spare battery.
3. Small first-aid pouch with dressings for minor human or equine injuries; serious treatment requires a medic or veterinarian.

## VI. VEHICLE

**Primary Mode of Transportation: Vesper, “Ves” — a black gelding.**

A sturdy, sure-footed draft-cross trail horse with a black coat, dark mane, and a small notch in his left ear. Byke calls him “the finest vehicle ever built” and immediately ruins the tough-guy effect by checking his hooves and sharing a piece of apple. Ves responds to patient handling and familiar cues; he is neither bulletproof nor immune to panic, and Byke avoids taking him into active gunfire.

Ves wears a practical dark leather saddle with saddlebags and a rolled rain sheet. He is Byke's first choice for trails, rural travel, and broken ground where a horse can safely pass. Black horses have been Byke's weakness since childhood, but this particular one earned his loyalty by carrying him home after a motorcycle spill left him too sore to walk.

For long journeys and team transport, Byke uses a battered diesel pickup and a serviceable horse trailer. He leaves Ves with a trusted handler when the job calls for engines, enclosed spaces, or likely combat. Fuel, maintenance, rest, water, and feed all remain real logistical needs.

## VII. FLAW/WEAKNESS

**“I can get us through.”** Byke ties his self-worth to being the person who can move anything and leave nobody stranded. A sneer about his competence, an unfamiliar machine, or a blocked route can tempt him to overpromise, hide his uncertainty, and keep trying after a sensible operator would stop. He is friendly with people who treat him as an equal, but becomes stubborn when a superior confuses authority with expertise.

In play, this can cost the team time, fuel, equipment, or the safer route. His usual mistake is committing before checking the limits; once someone is aboard, his affection for his passengers competes sharply with his pride. A teammate who gives him a specific task or an honest reason to back off can reach him more easily than someone who simply orders him to obey.

He will also risk his pay and his employer's goodwill to retrieve an abandoned teammate or animal. That makes him dependable company and a predictable target for anyone who knows what he cannot bear to leave behind.
